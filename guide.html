Margin.

A little app for catching words you don't know while you're reading — you're one of the first people trying it out.

Why "Margin"?

Before there was an app for this, readers did it by hand: an unfamiliar word, and a quick note on what it means, scribbled in the blank space next to the text — the margin of the page. This is that same habit, just faster, and it remembers what you wrote.

Thanks for poking at this. Margin is something I've been building: hit a word you don't know in a book, catch it, and keep it tagged to that book.

Four honest things first:

  • It's a personal project, not a product. Expect rough edges, and expect it to look different next week.
  • Sign in is an emailed link, no password. Margin is invite-only — if you're not on the list yet, the app offers to ask for you.
  • Free, no payment details, ever. Signing in only keeps your list yours.
  • What leaves your device, and only ever because you asked:
    • the word you look up → a free public dictionary
    • the word and your book title → Google's Gemini, if you open the AI tab
    • a page or cover photo → Google's Gemini, to be read as text and discarded
    • a scanned book's ISBN → Open Library, for the cover picture
    • the word you ask to hear → your phone's own voice, if the dictionary has no recording. Most phones read it out on the device; some Android voices fetch from Google instead
    • anything you write in Send feedback → me, with your email so I can reply and your browser so I can reproduce a bug
    Your saved words stay private to your account. Margin keeps no photos.

Get it on your phone

It installs like a regular app icon, but it's really a website — so "installing" just means adding it to your home screen from the browser.

iPhone & iPad

Must be opened in Safari — other browsers can't add home screen icons on iOS. The icon signs in separately from Safari, so expect to sign in once more inside it (use the code, see below).

  1. Open the link below in Safari.
  2. Tap the Share icon (square with an arrow).
  3. Scroll down and tap Add to Home Screen.
  4. Tap Add in the top right.

Android

Chrome is the most reliable browser for this. It may also just offer you an Install Margin button once the page loads — that does the same thing in one tap.

  1. Open the link below in Chrome.
  2. Tap the ⋮ menu, top right.
  3. Tap Add to Home screen (sometimes shown as Install app).
  4. Confirm by tapping Add / Install.

Trying it out

Open it from your home screen and it fills the screen, with no browser bar.

Signing in. It asks for your email and sends a link. Tap it and you're in, on that device, for good. If you haven't been added yet, it offers to ask for you — that's a real person approving it, so it isn't instant.

The same email also carries a numeric code, and you can type that into the app instead of tapping the link. Either one works; using one spends the other, and both expire, so use the newest email if you've asked more than once.

On an iPhone or iPad, the icon needs the code, not the link. Apple keeps a home screen app's sign-in separate from Safari's, and a link tapped in Mail always opens Safari — so it signs you in there rather than in the app, however many times you try. Open the icon, put your email in, then type the code from the email. Tap the code box and iOS usually offers the whole code just above the keyboard, so there's nothing to read off or remember. You only do this once.

If the email doesn't arrive, check spam. Margin's mail comes from an ordinary Gmail account, which is often enough to get the first one filed as junk. Mark it "Not spam" once and the rest come through.

Looking a word up. Type it, or tap the mic and say it — it searches as you type, so you needn't press anything. One meaning gives you one card; several give you one per sense, grouped by part of speech, so you save the one you actually met.

Scanning instead of typing. The scan button takes a photo of the page and lets you drag a box round the part you want. A tilted photo gets straightened before it's read, so the marks land squarely on the words.

Recognised words are drawn onto the photo itself rather than listed beside it. The ones worth looking up are underlined; everyday words — and, her, was — are left unmarked so the page doesn't come back as a wall of lines, though tapping one still works if you want it. Double-tap the photo to zoom in, and if a word comes out wrong, typing it is always there.

Reading, and the library. Whatever's in the "Reading" field gets tagged onto every word you save — that's what makes the list worth having later. The Library… dropdown keeps your books in one place; add one by typing the title or by scanning its cover.

The line from your book. An optional field on the result card for the sentence you actually found the word in.

The AI tab. Next to "Dictionary" on the card. It writes a definition aimed at whatever book you named, which helps with ambiguous words. A few seconds the first time, instant after. Entirely optional.

Your words, and practice. Saved words further down starts closed — tap it to open, then tap any word for its full entry and a Learned it button. Practice on the same row gives you flashcards or a quiz; the quiz needs four saved words, flashcards need one.

There's a light / dark / system toggle in the top corner.

Anything you liked, anything confusing or broken — there's a Send feedback button at the bottom of the app, and it comes straight to me.
