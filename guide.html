Margin.

A little app for catching words you don't know while you're reading — you're one of the first people trying it out.

Why "Margin"?

Before there was an app for this, readers did it by hand: an unfamiliar word, and a quick note on what it means, scribbled in the blank space next to the text — the margin of the page. This is that same habit, just faster, and it remembers what you wrote.

Hi — thanks for agreeing to poke at this. Margin is something I've been building myself: when you hit a word you don't know in a book, you type it, say it, or point your camera at the page, get a definition, and keep it tagged to that book so you can look back later.

A few honest things before you install it:

  • This is a personal project, not a finished product — expect rough edges, and things may look different next week as I keep working on it.
  • You'll sign in with just your email — a link we send you, no password. Margin is invite-only, so if your address isn't on the list yet the app won't just fail at you: it'll offer to ask on your behalf. Leave your name and you'll get an email once I've added you. Once you're in, your saved words follow your account across devices.
  • It's free, no payment details — ever. Signing in is just to keep your list yours and separate from anyone else testing this.
  • Your saved words are stored privately under your account, not shared with other testers. Word lookups go to a free public dictionary by default. Three other things leave your device, and only ever because you asked for them: the optional "AI" tab sends that word and whatever book you typed to Google's Gemini; scanning a page or a book cover sends that photo to the same place to be read back as text; and a scanned book's ISBN goes to Open Library to fetch its cover picture. Nothing else about you or your device goes along with any of it, and Margin doesn't keep the photos — they're read and thrown away.

Get it on your phone

It installs like a regular app icon, but it's really a website — so "installing" just means adding it to your home screen from the browser.

iPhone & iPad

Must be opened in Safari — other browsers can't add home screen icons on iOS.

  1. Open the link below in Safari.
  2. Tap the Share icon (square with an arrow).
  3. Scroll down and tap Add to Home Screen.
  4. Tap Add in the top right.

Android

Chrome is the most reliable browser for this. It may also just offer you an Install Margin button once the page loads — that does the same thing in one tap.

  1. Open the link below in Chrome.
  2. Tap the ⋮ menu, top right.
  3. Tap Add to Home screen (sometimes shown as Install app).
  4. Confirm by tapping Add / Install.

Trying it out

Once it's on your home screen, open it like any app — it'll fill the screen with no browser bar around it.

First time in, it'll ask for your email. If you've already been added — which you have, if an email invited you here — a sign-in link lands in your inbox. Tap it, the app reopens signed in, and you won't need to do this again on that device.

If you've only got the link and haven't been added yet, the app will say so and offer to ask for you: leave your name and a line about how we know each other, and you'll get an email once I've approved it. That last part is a real person tapping a button, so it won't be instant.

Type (or tap the mic and speak) a word you've come across recently, add the title of whatever you were reading in the "Reading" field, and hit Look up — or just pause, since it searches as you type. Some words only have one meaning — that's a single card with a Save to list button. Others have several ("rubric" has ten!) — you'll see them grouped by part of speech, each with its own Save this meaning button, so you can pick whichever one actually matches how you saw it used. That's really it — the more real words you try, the more useful your feedback is.

Typing a long word off a page is the tedious part, so there's a third option next to the mic: the scan button. Point the camera at the page (or pick a photo you've already taken), drag a box over the paragraph you care about, and it reads the words back to you as a list — tap the one you were after. If it misreads something, there's an "Enter it manually instead" way out.

The Reading field remembers books you've used before, and the Library… dropdown beside it keeps them in one place. You can add a book by typing its title, or by scanning its cover — that fills in the title, author and cover picture for you. Whatever's in "Reading" gets tagged onto each word you save, which is what makes the saved list worth having later.

When you save a word there's also an optional line from your book field — the actual sentence you found it in. It's the difference between a definition you have to take on faith and one you can see working.

Next to "Dictionary" on the result card there's an AI tab — tap it for an AI-written definition instead, which tries to match the sense of the word to whatever book you typed in "Reading." It takes a few seconds the first time (it's asking an AI model, not just looking a word up), then it's instant if you switch back and forth. Totally optional — the free dictionary is the default and works fine without it.

Your saved words live under Saved words further down, with a count next to it. It starts closed — tap the heading to open it, then tap any word to see its full entry, the book it came from, and a Learned it button for when you've got one down for good. Learned words stay in the list, greyed out, but stop coming up in practice.

Speaking of which: Practice sits on that same row, to the right. Review as flashcards (tap a card to flip it and reveal the meaning), or as a quick multiple-choice quiz. Quiz mode needs at least 4 saved words to have enough wrong answers to choose from; flashcards work with just one. If you've filtered to one book, you'll practise only that book's words.

There's also a small light / dark / system icon in the top corner if the default look isn't to your taste.

Questions, confusions, or things that broke — just tell whoever sent you this. That's exactly what this round of testing is for.
