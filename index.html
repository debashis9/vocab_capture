Short answers on how Margin works, and what it does with your things.

What this is

What is Margin?
A place to put the words you meet while reading. You're on a page, you hit a word you don't know, and by the time you've found a dictionary the sentence has gone cold — Margin is the shortest path from that word to its meaning, and it keeps the word afterwards, filed under the book you found it in. Type it, say it, or photograph the page and tap the word. Later, the words you've kept turn into flashcards and a quiz. It's a small personal project, not a service, which is why it's invite-only and why there are no accounts to manage, no adverts and nothing to pay.

Looking a word up

Where do the definitions come from?
A free public dictionary API built on Wiktionary's data. No account or key needed, and it's what every lookup uses by default.
Why does one word show several meanings?
Because most words have several. Margin shows every sense, grouped by part of speech, and lets you save the one that matches how you actually saw it used rather than guessing on your behalf.
What is the AI tab?
An optional second opinion. It asks an AI model for a definition written for whatever book is in your “Reading” field, so an ambiguous word gets the sense that fits. It only runs when you open the tab, never on every lookup.
The word wasn't found — is that a dead end?
No. The card still appears with the AI tab a tap away, which is usually the answer for names, slang and very new words. If you're offline you'll get a “Save for later” button instead.
Can I say the word instead of typing it?
Yes — the mic button uses your device's own speech recognition.

Reading off the page

What does the scan button do?
Takes a photo of the page — or uses one you already have — and lets you drag a box around the part you care about. Every word in it becomes tappable, appearing as they're recognised.
What happens to the photo?
It's sent to Google's Gemini to be read back as text, then discarded. Margin doesn't keep photos.
Can I scan a book instead of typing its title?
Yes. Scan the cover and it fills in the title, author and ISBN; the cover picture comes from Open Library. There's a manual entry fallback if the cover is damaged or too shiny to read.
What is “the line from your book”?
An optional field for the actual sentence you met the word in — which is a different thing from the dictionary's own example sentence.

Your words

Where are my saved words kept?
In your own account, so they follow you from your phone to any other device you sign in on.
Can anyone else see them?
No. Every saved word is tied to your account, and the database enforces that itself rather than trusting the app to.
What does “Learned it” do?
Marks a word as one you've got. It stays in your list, greyed out, and stops coming up in practice.
How does practice work?
Flashcards you tap to flip, or a quick multiple-choice quiz. Both are built from your own saved words — no AI, no connection needed. Filter to one book first and you'll practise only that book. The quiz needs at least four saved words to have enough wrong answers to offer.

Offline, updates and access

Does it work without a connection?
Your saved words and practice do, from a copy kept on the device. Looking up a new word needs the network — but you can queue one with “Save for later” and it resolves itself once you're back online.
Why did a “new version” bar appear?
The app updated itself in the background. Tapping Refresh loads the new one; nothing is lost either way.
Why is signing in invite-only?
It's a personal project rather than a service. If you have the link but no account yet, the app will offer to ask on your behalf.
The sign-in email never arrived.
Check your spam folder. Margin's mail comes from an ordinary personal Gmail account, which is often enough for it to be filtered the first time. Mark it “Not spam” once and the rest arrive normally.
