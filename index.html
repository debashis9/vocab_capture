Common questions

What this is

What is Margin?
A place to keep the words you meet while reading. Catch one off the page — type it, say it, or photograph it — and it's filed under the book you found it in. Later they become flashcards and a quiz.

Looking a word up

Where do the definitions come from?
Free public dictionaries built on Wiktionary's data.
Why does one word show several meanings?
Most words have several. Margin shows them all, grouped by part of speech, so you save the one you actually met.
What is the AI tab?
A second opinion, written for whatever book is in your “Reading” field — handy when a word is ambiguous. It starts working as soon as you look a word up, so it's usually ready even when the dictionary is being slow. A small dot on the tab means its answer is waiting.
I mistyped a word — will it still work?
Usually. If the spelling is close, Margin offers the word it thinks you meant; tap it for the full dictionary entry. Saving from the AI tab files the corrected spelling, not the typo.
The word wasn't found — is that a dead end?
No. The card still appears with the AI tab beside it, which usually handles names, slang and new words. Offline, you get “Save for later” instead.
Can I say the word instead of typing it?
Yes — the mic uses your device's own speech recognition.

Reading off the page

What does the scan button do?
Photograph the page and drag a box around the part you want. Words appear on the photo as they're recognised — underlined ones are worth looking up, everyday words are left unmarked but still tap. Double-tap to zoom.
Can I scan a book instead of typing its title?
Yes — scan the cover for title and author. Manual entry is there if the cover won't read.
What is “the line from your book”?
The actual sentence you met the word in, as opposed to the dictionary's own example.

Your words

Where are my saved words kept?
In your account, so they follow you between devices. Nobody else can see them.
Why is “Reading” empty when I open the app?
On purpose. It holds while you're using the app, so several words from one book need it typed once — but it starts empty each time you open, because the commonest way to file a word under the wrong book is an old title still sitting there. The × clears it any time, which is what you want when you put one book down mid-chapter and pick another up.
What does “Learned it” do?
Marks a word as got. It stays in the list, greyed out, and drops out of practice.
How does practice work?
Flashcards to flip, or a multiple-choice quiz — both built from your own saved words, no connection needed. Filter to a book first to practise just that one. The quiz needs four saved words.

Offline, updates and access

Does it work without a connection?
Saved words and practice do, from a local copy. New lookups need the network, but “Save for later” queues one and resolves it when you're back.
Why did a “new version” bar appear?
The app updated in the background. Refresh loads it; nothing is lost either way.
Why is signing in invite-only?
It's a personal project, not a service. With the link but no account, the app offers to ask for you.
The sign-in email never arrived.
Check spam. Margin's mail comes from a personal Gmail account, so it's often filtered the first time. Mark it “Not spam” once and the rest arrive.
Why is there a code as well as a link?
Either one signs you in, and using one spends the other. On iPhone and iPad, a home screen icon can't be signed in by a tapped link — only the code works there. It's also the way past a link that landed in spam.

Privacy

What leaves my device?
Only what a lookup needs, and only because you asked:
  • the word you look up → the dictionaries
  • the word and your book title → Google's AI, so its answer is ready if you want it
  • a page or cover photo → Google's AI, to be read as text and discarded
  • a scanned book's ISBN → Open Library, for the cover picture
  • anything you write in Send feedback → me, with your email so I can reply
Margin keeps no photos, and your saved words stay private to your account.
